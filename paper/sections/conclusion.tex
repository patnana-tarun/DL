\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We studied where a pseudo-supervised, VLM-enabled crowd-analytics pipeline fails and redesigned it accordingly.
Pseudo-label fine-tuning of a compact detector on one camera improved that camera and damaged every other one,
including human-labelled COCO people; the apparent CNN-over-transformer advantage was an input-size effect; and
frame-level statistics understated uncertainty by a factor of about \TcAaRatioMin--\TcAaRatioMax. A WiSE blend chosen
on other cameras' validation data recovered held-out accuracy (\MsSelHo{} vs.\ \MsFtHo{} for the original choice) at
no cost, and a trusted policy layer raised the rejection of unsafe VLM requests from \SfGrTestRej{} to
\SfPoTestRej{} without hurting legitimate ones. Independent human ground truth remains open and is prepared (protocol, tool and analysis
released).

\textbf{Future work.} The most important next step is on-device validation on a Jetson Orin: end-to-end frame
latency with its tail (p95/p99) and deadline misses, power and energy per frame, thermal drift over long runs, and the
contention between the detector and the VLM sharing one GPU and 8\,GB of unified memory, measured with TensorRT
engines over runs of at least ten minutes~\cite{reddi2020mlperf}. Beyond that, the policy layer should be tested
against adaptive red-teaming, and the scene-shift study extended to more cameras, night scenes and weather. All numbers are
generated from the result files (\texttt{paper/make\_numbers.py}); \texttt{REPRODUCE.md} maps every table and figure
to its command, and \texttt{MANIFEST.sha256} pins every input and artifact.
