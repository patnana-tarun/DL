\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Detectors and trackers}
One-stage YOLO detectors~\cite{redmon2016yolo} are the usual choice for embedded deployment. YOLOv10~\cite{wang2024yolov10}
introduced consistent dual assignments for NMS-free inference, and YOLO26~\cite{jocher2026yolo26,ultralytics} ships both a
one-to-one and a one-to-many head. DETR~\cite{carion2020detr} removed hand-crafted post-processing, RT-DETR~\cite{zhao2024rtdetr}
made it real-time, and RF-DETR~\cite{robinson2026rfdetr} adds a DINOv2 backbone~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} with a native Nano
resolution of 384\,px. Detection is scored with COCO AP~\cite{lin2014coco} and, for pedestrians, the log-average miss
rate~\cite{dollar2012pedestrian,zhang2017citypersons}. We use SORT-family trackers~\cite{bewley2016sort,zhang2022bytetrack,cao2023ocsort,aharon2022botsort,yang2023cbiou,stanczyk2024mcbyte}
scored with HOTA~\cite{luiten2021hota}, CLEAR-MOT~\cite{bernardin2008clear} and IDF1~\cite{ristani2016idf1}.

\subsection{Pseudo-labels, scene adaptation and robust fine-tuning}
Distillation~\cite{hinton2015distilling}, data distillation~\cite{radosavovic2018datadistill} and Noisy
Student~\cite{xie2020noisystudent} train a student on teacher labels; we fuse two teacher families with weighted box
fusion~\cite{solovyev2021wbf}. Scenes100~\cite{zhang2023scenes100} is the closest work. It adapts a detector to each of
100 fixed cameras with self-generated pseudo-labels, mixes COCO images in one-to-one, and reports gains on the adapted
camera. It does not report what the adapted model loses on other cameras, which is what we measure. Fine-tuning is
known to distort pretrained features and hurt out-of-distribution accuracy in classification~\cite{kumar2022lpft} and in
detection backbones~\cite{saito2023mindbackbone}. The remedies include regularising towards the pretrained
weights~\cite{li2018l2sp}, linear probing before fine-tuning~\cite{kumar2022lpft} and weight-space ensembling
(WiSE-FT~\cite{wortsman2022wise}). WiSE-OD~\cite{medeiros2026wiseod} applied it to detectors, YOLOv8n among them, for
transfer from RGB to infrared with human-labelled infrared data, and left BatchNorm handling open. In our setting
the fine-tuning labels are pseudo-labels from one camera, the mixing coefficient has to be chosen without data from
the target camera, and we measure how the BatchNorm statistics should be merged. Robustness is judged against the linear trend between in- and
out-of-distribution accuracy~\cite{taori2020shift}.

\subsection{Statistics on video frames}
Bootstrap intervals over test images are standard in detection~\cite{everingham2015voc}, but frames from one video are
dependent. Block bootstraps~\cite{kunsch1989,politis1992cbb} resample contiguous blocks, and the block length can be
chosen from the data~\cite{politis2004block,patton2009correction}. Monte-Carlo $p$-values need a finite-sample
correction~\cite{phipson2010pvalues}, and dependent test items invalidate the usual significance
tests~\cite{dror2018hitchhiker}. We apply these tools to detection AP, which is not a per-frame mean.

\subsection{Tool-using VLMs and prompt injection}
Instruction-tuned VLMs~\cite{liu2023llava} such as Qwen2.5-VL~\cite{bai2025qwen25vl} and Qwen3-VL~\cite{bai2025qwen3vl}
answer free-form questions about images, and language models can call tools~\cite{schick2023toolformer,yao2023react}.
AgentDojo~\cite{debenedetti2024agentdojo} and InjecAgent~\cite{zhan2024injecagent} measure prompt injection through tool
results, and FigStep~\cite{gong2025figstep} shows that text rendered in an image jailbreaks open VLMs. Defences that
hold by construction separate the model that reads untrusted content from the one that acts, or let a fixed
policy decide every action~\cite{beurerkellner2025patterns,debenedetti2025camel,owasp2025llm}. We apply these design
patterns to an operator agent whose untrusted input is a live camera, and measure the result.
