\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper showed that pseudo-label fine-tuning of a compact person detector on a single camera improves accuracy on
that camera but reduces it below the pretrained baseline on every other camera tested and on COCO person images. The
loss is attributable to a size-blind pseudo-label threshold and to forgetting. Averaging the pretrained and fine-tuned
weights, with the coefficient selected on other cameras, restored the test-only accuracy (\MsSelHo{} compared with
\MsFtHo{} for the model selected on the adaptation camera) at no additional cost. The apparent accuracy advantage of
the CNN student was found to be an effect of input resolution. For the VLM operator, text in the camera view
constitutes an attack channel, and a policy layer outside the model raised the rejection rate of unsafe requests from
\SfGrTestRej{} to \SfPoTestRej{} without affecting legitimate requests. Future work comprises the human ground-truth
evaluation, on-device measurements on a Jetson Orin, adaptive red-teaming of the policy layer, and evaluation on
additional cameras, including night-time scenes. All numbers in the paper are generated from the released result
files by \texttt{paper/make\_numbers.py}.
