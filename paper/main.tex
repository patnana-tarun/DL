\begin{abstract}
Crowd-analytics systems are commonly adapted to a new camera by pseudo-labelling its footage with large teacher
detectors and fine-tuning a compact student detector on the result. Prior work reports the resulting gain on the
adapted camera only. This paper quantifies the cost of such adaptation on other cameras and proposes a mitigation
that requires no target-camera labels. Fine-tuning \yolo{} on pseudo-labels from a single plaza video raises its
\AP{} on that video from \MsPtAa{} to \MsFtAa{}, but lowers it on all three other cameras and on human-labelled COCO
person images; on a test-only video it falls from \MsPtHo{} to \MsFtHo{}, below the pretrained weights. Part of this
loss is caused by the pseudo-label score threshold, which discards \LtAaA{} of the teacher boxes on people shorter
than 32\,px and thereby trains the student to treat distant people as background; the remainder is attributed to
forgetting. Averaging the pretrained and fine-tuned weights, with the mixing coefficient selected on validation data
from other cameras, retains most of the in-domain gain (\MsSelAa) and restores the test-only accuracy (\MsSelHo)
without additional training or inference cost, whereas re-estimating the BatchNorm statistics after averaging
degrades accuracy. A resolution-controlled comparison shows that \rfdetr{} matches \yolo{} on the adapted camera at
equal input size and transfers considerably better. The selected detector drives a streaming runtime in which
Qwen3-VL-2B answers operator queries and modifies alert rules through tool calls. The model reproduces any count it
is given, confirms fabricated alerts, and executes instructions painted on a sign in the camera view once that text
enters the chat history. A deterministic policy layer outside the model increases the rejection rate of unsafe
requests on a 300-case adversarial suite from \SfGrTestRej{} to \SfPoTestRej{} without reducing task success.
\end{abstract}
