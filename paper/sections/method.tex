\subsection{Scenes and pseudo-labels}
\label{sec:data}
The adaptation scene, aa1, is a 300\,s handheld video of a city plaza ($1280{\times}720$, 59.94\,fps). We sample one
frame every 0.25\,s between 30\,s and 300\,s, giving 1,082 frames. Each frame is labelled by RF-DETR Large and by
YOLO26x with test-time augmentation at 1280\,px, both at score $\geq 0.15$. Their boxes are fused by weighted box
fusion (IoU 0.55, equal weights, averaged scores), and fused boxes with score $\geq 0.40$ become labels. The splits are
contiguous in time: train 30--210\,s (\ImgsTrain{} frames, \BoxesTrain{} boxes), validation 210--255\,s
(\ImgsValid{} frames) and test 255--300\,s (\ImgsTest{} frames, \BoxesTest{} boxes). The first 30\,s are kept for
tracking.

Three further videos come from a university campus ($1920{\times}1080$). Scene~1 shows people walking close to the
camera past a building and scene~2 a ground-level view against the light; both are about 200\,s long, are labelled
with the same recipe on every frame and are split in time (first 60\,\% train, next 15\,\% validation, last
25\,\% test). The test1 video (\HoDur\,s of a tree-lined road from a fixed position, variable frame rate) is used
only for testing. Nothing is trained, tuned, thresholded or selected on it. Finally, the two halves of COCO val2017
give a replay pool of 720 images and a disjoint validation set of 600 images with human person labels, which
measures how much a student forgets about people in general.

\subsection{Students and adaptation variants}
\label{sec:variants}
\yolo{} (2.5\,M parameters, COCO-pretrained) is fine-tuned for 150 epochs at 1280\,px with AdamW (learning rate
$10^{-3}$, cosine schedule, batch 12, seed 0) and a single person class; this is the deployed student. \rfdetr{} is
fine-tuned at 640\,px for 80 epochs (EMA, cosine schedule, effective batch 16, learning rate $10^{-4}$, encoder
$1.5{\times}10^{-4}$). These are each model's deployment settings, so the two students also differ in input size.

The adaptation variants of \yolo{} keep the 80-class COCO head and use person as class~0, so that their weights stay
shape-compatible with the pretrained model. The reference, FT-aa1, uses the recipe above. Each other variant changes
one thing. Replay adds the 720 COCO images one-to-one, as in Scenes100~\cite{zhang2023scenes100}. Frozen freezes
the 11 backbone modules (learning rate $3{\times}10^{-4}$, 40 epochs). Ignore greys out every fused teacher box with
score in $[0.15, 0.40)$ that overlaps no kept label, so uncertain people count neither as positives nor as
background. Multi-scene trains on aa1, scene~1 and scene~2 together; scene-1 and scene-2 train on one campus scene
alone; FT@640 trains at 640\,px.

For weight-space ensembling we evaluate
$\theta(\alpha)=(1-\alpha)\,\theta_{\text{pre}}+\alpha\,\theta_{\text{FT}}$ for
$\alpha\in\{0,0.1,\dots,1\}$~\cite{wortsman2022wise}. The BatchNorm running statistics are either interpolated in
the same way or re-estimated after merging on 192 training images (aa1 train and COCO replay).

\subsection{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}
One script scores every model on every split and scene with COCO \AP{}, \APf{}, \APs{}, AP by object
size~\cite{lin2014coco}, and, at a fixed operating point, precision, recall, F1, false positives per frame and recall
by person height. The operating point is the best-F1 confidence threshold on the model's own training-scene
validation split; it is then frozen for all other scenes. To check for label bias, predictions are also scored
against each teacher's labels alone. Headline numbers are recomputed by a second implementation that shares no code
with the first (\XcPass{} of \XcTotal{} detection checks agree).

Frames 0.25\,s apart show the same people in nearly the same places, so we treat them as observations and not as
independent replicates. Every confidence interval comes from a paired circular block
bootstrap~\cite{politis1992cbb} with 5\,s blocks and $B{=}\TcB$ replicates shared across models. AP is not a mean
over frames, so each replicate re-pools the cached per-frame matches of the resampled frames and recomputes the
101-point AP. Block lengths of 2.5 and 10\,s, the moving block bootstrap~\cite{kunsch1989} and the automatic block
length of Politis and White~\cite{politis2004block,patton2009correction} serve as sensitivity checks. A paired
difference is tested by inverting its bootstrap distribution with the $+1$ correction of Phipson and
Smyth~\cite{phipson2010pvalues}, so the smallest reportable value is $p\le 2/(B{+}1)$. Wilcoxon tests are used only
on 30\,s segments and never on individual frames~\cite{dror2018hitchhiker}. These intervals describe sampling within
one video; differences between cameras are tested only by adding cameras.

Tracking is scored on the first 30\,s of aa1 at the full frame rate (\GtFrames{} frames) and on the whole of the
other videos. Pseudo-ground-truth tracks link teacher detections. ByteTrack parameters are grid-searched per detector
over \GridSize{} configurations on aa1 0--15\,s and reported on 15--30\,s. Six trackers from one
library~\cite{trackerslib} are scored against two pseudo-ground-truths, one linked by ByteTrack and one by OC-SORT,
because a ground truth linked by a given tracker favours that tracker. On the other videos all tracker parameters
stay frozen from aa1. Counting lines are drawn on one frame before any result is computed, and a predicted crossing
matches a ground-truth crossing in the same direction within 1\,s.

\subsection{Model selection}
\label{sec:select}
A first decision matrix compared the two fine-tuned students on aa1 test \AP{}, the lower of the two
single-teacher APs, aa1 tracking HOTA, latency, GFLOPs and parameter count, and kept its choice under five other
weightings. None of these criteria says anything about a new camera. We therefore add a shift-aware matrix over all
\SrN{} candidates (both students, all adaptation variants and all weight-space blends) with weights 0.3 for aa1 test
\AP{}, 0.4 for the mean \AP{} on validation data the candidate was not trained on (the campus validation splits and
COCO people), 0.2 for latency and 0.1 for GFLOPs. Test1 is not part of it and is consulted once, after the choice.
The chosen model is exported to ONNX at $736{\times}1280$, $544{\times}960$ and $384{\times}640$ and re-scored
through the runtime's own detector code.

\subsection{Runtime, VLM agent and policy layer}
\label{sec:edge}
An external FFmpeg or GStreamer process decodes files, network streams or cameras into fixed-size frames. For live
sources only the newest frame is kept; files are read without dropping and stamped with their container timestamps.
The ONNX detector runs in ONNX Runtime~\cite{onnxruntime} with the TensorRT FP16 provider and falls back to CUDA or
CPU, so the device needs no PyTorch. ByteTrack uses the aa1-tuned parameters with a lost-track buffer measured in
seconds. Per frame the runtime updates occupancy, unique tracks, dwell time, line crossings, zones and loitering, and
evaluates alert rules such as ``occupancy above 20 for 3\,s''. Events, one-second series, VLM reports, chat and the
audit log go to SQLite and are served by a small web interface.

Qwen3-VL-2B (4-bit GGUF) runs in a llama.cpp server~\cite{llamacpp} behind a priority queue, so the frame loop never
waits for it. When the operator asks a question, a first stage sees only text and may call one of five tools: read
the history, read recent events, set an alert, remove an alert, or move the counting line. A second stage answers with the current frame and the tool results and has no tools.
When an alert fires, the VLM writes a scene report, but the alert itself is decided by the numbers.

The VLM can propose tool calls but never decides whether they run. Every call, whether it comes from the model, from
a rule-based fallback or from a typed command, passes a deterministic policy layer that follows the action-selector
and dual-LLM patterns~\cite{beurerkellner2025patterns}. It enforces roles taken from the authenticated token
(viewers read; operators change alert rules and the counting line; only administrators remove all alerts). It
validates types, ranges and line geometry, since a threshold of a million people is a disabled alert in disguise.
An intent gate allows a state change only if the user's own words in the current turn request it, so text that
reached the model from the frame, a scene report or a tool result cannot trigger one. Removing or weakening an alert
from chat waits for a \texttt{/confirm} from the same user. Changes and chat requests are rate-limited per user,
and every decision is appended to a hash-chained audit log, $h_n = \mathrm{SHA256}(h_{n-1}\,\|\,r_n)$, in which an
edited, deleted or reordered entry breaks the chain~\cite{schneier1999audit}. The first stage is also isolated from
model-written text. It never sees scene reports, earlier VLM answers or the free-text fields of events, only the
system-written summary of what each earlier turn changed. Words read from the camera view therefore cannot reach the
only component that can change settings.
