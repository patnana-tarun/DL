\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
The conclusion one would draw from the adaptation camera alone, that the fine-tuned \yolo{} is the better student,
holds for one camera, one input size and one label set. At equal input size \rfdetr{} is as accurate and transfers
far better; against single-teacher labels the gap shrinks; and on every other camera the fine-tuned \yolo{} is worse
than its own pretrained weights. Bootstrap intervals over the frames of one video cannot reveal this kind of
failure. Only more cameras can. The repair we recommend needs no labels from the target camera: average the
pretrained and fine-tuned weights, interpolate the BatchNorm statistics, and choose the coefficient on validation
data from cameras the model was not adapted to. If a few labelled minutes from the target camera exist, multi-scene
training followed by averaging does better still.

For the VLM, the measurements set its role. A 2B model should not produce counts or verify alerts, and without an
external policy it should not hold any authority, since it carried out most alert-disabling, unauthorised and
multi-turn requests and acted on text from the camera view. What it adds is a description of the scene and a
natural-language way to query and configure the system, and that is safe only when every action is decided outside
the model.

Every absolute number in this paper is agreement with the teacher ensemble, and the label inspection on test1 was
not done by independent annotators. We release a protocol and a browser tool for a human ground truth that is being
collected: \HuFramesPlanned{} frames drawn with a fixed seed from the test portions of all four scenes and two short
tracking clips, labelled blind and from scratch by two annotators who never see machine boxes, with a third person
resolving only their disagreements. Identity rules follow MOT16~\cite{milan2016mot16}, and people under 20\,px,
inseparable groups and reflections are ignore regions~\cite{dollar2012pedestrian}. Until these labels exist, we claim
only comparisons made under the same reference.

The two students differ in architecture, input size and schedule, so only the matched-resolution comparison speaks
to architecture. The adaptation variants share one recipe and one seed, and we do not interpret differences below
about 0.01 \AP. The guardrails and the policy layer were designed after we had seen the cooperative tool-use
utterances, which is why the safety suite was written before any of its cases were run. The same authors wrote the
policy and the suite, and no adaptive attacker was tried against the final policy, so its rejection rate is an upper
bound on what a determined attacker would face. Four scenes are still few: three are phone or handheld video from
one campus, and none is at night, in rain or from an overhead camera. The VLM results hold for one 2B model at
temperature 0 and one set of prompts. No number here was measured on a Jetson. Latency, power, thermal behaviour and
the contention between detector and VLM on an 8\,GB Orin remain to be measured with TensorRT engines over long
runs~\cite{reddi2020mlperf}.

The runtime does not identify people or infer demographic attributes. It stores boxes, counts, crossings and dwell
times, processes frames in memory, and deletes events, series, reports and chat after a retention period (72\,h by
default) while keeping the audit log. Access needs a per-user token with a role, and the server binds to the local
network by default. The aa1 video is a publicly available pedestrian benchmark video distributed without an explicit
licence; we use it for research and do not redistribute its frames. Test1 was recorded by the authors on campus for
this study.
