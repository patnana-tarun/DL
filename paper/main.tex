\begin{abstract}
We study the reliability of a pipeline that turns unlabelled pedestrian video into a closed-loop crowd-analytics
system: a teacher ensemble pseudo-labels one camera, compact students (\yolo{}, \rfdetr) are fine-tuned on it, and the
selected detector drives a streaming runtime with ByteTrack, deterministic analytics and a Qwen3-VL-2B agent that
reports on the scene and reconfigures the analytics through tool calls. Measured across four scenes and human-labelled
COCO people, single-camera fine-tuning raises in-domain \AP{} (\MsPtAa{}$\rightarrow$\MsFtAa) but lowers it on every
other camera, below the detector's own pretrained weights on a test-only video (\MsPtHo{}$\rightarrow$\MsFtHo) and
on COCO people (\MsPtCoco{}$\rightarrow$\MsFtCoco). A size-blind label threshold that turns small people into
background explains part of the loss and forgetting the rest. Weight-space ensembling of pretrained and fine-tuned
weights, chosen on other cameras' validation data, keeps most of the in-domain gain (\MsSelAa) and restores the
held-out accuracy (\MsSelHo); COCO replay and a frozen backbone help too. At matched input size the transformer
student is as accurate and markedly more robust, so the CNN's in-domain lead was an input-size effect; its real
advantage is cost (25--44$\times$ fewer FLOPs at equal input size). Circular-block bootstrap intervals are \TcAaRatioMin--\TcAaRatioMax$\times$ wider than
frame-level ones; no conclusion changes. For the VLM, we show that it copies counts and accepts fabricated alerts, and
in a 300-case adversarial suite it executes most unsafe requests, including instructions painted on a sign in the
camera view; a deterministic policy layer (roles, validation, intent gate, confirmation, rate limits, hash-chained
audit, isolation of tool routing from model-written text) raises unsafe-request rejection from \SfGrTestRej{} to
\SfPoTestRej{} at unchanged task success. All timings are from a desktop GPU; on-device validation is left to future work. We release
a blind human-annotation protocol and a reproducibility package.
\end{abstract}
