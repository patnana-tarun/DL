\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
Evaluated on the adaptation camera alone, the fine-tuned \yolo{} appears to be the better student. This conclusion
holds for one camera, one input size and one label set only. At equal input size \rfdetr{} is equally accurate and
transfers considerably better; against single-teacher labels the gap narrows; and on every other camera the
fine-tuned \yolo{} is less accurate than its own pretrained weights. Bootstrap intervals computed over the frames of
a single video cannot reveal this type of failure, which becomes visible only with additional cameras. The
recommended mitigation requires no target-camera labels: the pretrained and fine-tuned weights are averaged, the
BatchNorm statistics are interpolated, and the coefficient is selected on validation data from cameras not used for
adaptation. When a few labelled minutes from the target camera are available, multi-scene training followed by
averaging performs better still.

The measurements also define the role of the VLM. A 2B model should not be used to produce counts or verify alerts,
and without an external policy it should not hold any authority, since it executed most alert-disabling,
unauthorised and multi-turn requests and acted on text from the camera view. Its contribution is scene description
and natural-language access to the system, which is safe only when every action is decided outside the model.

All absolute numbers in this paper measure agreement with the teacher ensemble, and the label inspection on test1 was
not performed by independent annotators. A protocol and a browser-based annotation tool for a human ground truth are
released, and annotation is in progress: \HuFramesPlanned{} frames sampled with a fixed seed from the test portions
of all four scenes and two short tracking clips are labelled blind and from scratch by two annotators who never see
machine boxes, and a third person resolves only their disagreements. Identity rules follow
MOT16~\cite{milan2016mot16}, and people under 20\,px, inseparable groups and reflections are treated as ignore
regions~\cite{dollar2012pedestrian}. Until these labels are available, only comparisons made under the same reference
are claimed.

Several limitations apply. The two students differ in architecture, input size and schedule, so only the
resolution-matched comparison isolates the effect of architecture. The adaptation variants share one recipe and one
seed, and differences below about 0.01 \AP{} are not interpreted. The guardrails and the policy layer were designed
after the cooperative tool-use utterances had been observed; the safety suite was therefore written before any of its
cases were run. The policy and the suite were written by the same authors, and no adaptive attacker was evaluated
against the final policy, so the reported rejection rate is an upper bound on the protection against a determined
attacker. Four scenes remain a small sample: three are handheld or phone recordings, three were captured on
one campus, and none covers night, rain or an overhead viewpoint. The VLM results apply to one 2B model at temperature 0 with one set of
prompts. Finally, no measurement was made on a Jetson; latency, power, thermal behaviour and the contention between
detector and VLM on an 8\,GB Orin remain to be measured with TensorRT engines over long runs~\cite{reddi2020mlperf}.

The runtime neither identifies individuals nor infers demographic attributes. It stores boxes, counts, crossings and
dwell times, processes frames in memory, and deletes events, time series, reports and chat after a retention period
(72\,h by default) while retaining the audit log. Access requires a per-user token with an assigned role, and the
server binds to the local network by default. The aa1 video is a publicly available pedestrian benchmark video
distributed without an explicit licence; it is used here for research only, and its frames are not redistributed.
Test1 was recorded by the authors on the university campus for this study, and scenes 1 and 2 are phone
recordings from the same campus.
